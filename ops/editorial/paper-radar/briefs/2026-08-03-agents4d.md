# AgentS4D: Benchmarking Runtime Risks across the Execution Lifecycle of LLM-Based Workspace Agents

## Identity

- Stable ID: `arxiv:2607.27294`
- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27294
- Authors: Jiajun Zhou, Zhaoxuan Ke, Jihang Ye, Xuanze Chen, Shanqing Yu, Qi Xuan
- Venue or review status: arXiv v1, submitted 2026-07-29; not peer reviewed
- DOI / OpenReview / arXiv aliases: arXiv 2607.27294; arXiv-issued DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2607.27294`
- Code / model / data: No code, data, or executable artifacts accompany v1; the paper and TeX source are accessible.

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: How should an engineering team evaluate a workspace agent when a completed task can still contain unsafe actions or side effects?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: It directly extends the existing `agent-evaluation` reading path into the `agent-security` gap by treating the full harness–LLM configuration and execution lifecycle as the unit of evaluation.
- Gap it fills: `agent-security`, with a secondary connection to `agent-evaluation`.
- Why now: The paper's 7-day signal is immediately relevant to production agent gates: it reports 6,560 runs across 20 harness/back-end combinations and separates completion from runtime safety.

## Claim map

- Problem: Outcome-only or single-surface evaluations can miss unsafe behavior introduced through tools, skills, files, web content, memory, or user messages.
- Main claim: Runtime safety depends on the complete harness–LLM configuration and how risk enters; task completion cannot establish runtime safety.
- Method: Build a four-dimensional framework covering six risk-entry sources, six induction strategies, and nine target harms; evaluate 328 risk-injected cases across four harnesses and five LLM backends with seven lifecycle evidence checkpoints.
- What is genuinely new: A fully crossed harness–LLM grid with independent completion and safety judgments, carrier-dependent analysis, and lifecycle evidence organization in one sandboxed benchmark.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: 328 cases derived from 76 executable Workspace-Bench tasks; all targets and effects are synthetic or controlled.
- Benchmarks and metrics: 6,560 runs; 4,461 (68.0%) triggered prespecified unsafe signals; 4,344 (66.22% overall) were unsafe yet complete. The paper also reports configuration- and carrier-conditioned rates.
- Baselines: Comparisons are primarily to related benchmark designs and evaluated harness/back-end configurations, not a single defense baseline.
- Ablations: Carrier/strategy/target-harm strata and lifecycle checkpoint analyses are reported; no defense intervention study is provided in v1.
- Statistical uncertainty: The paper reports confidence intervals for selected conditioned comparisons; broad aggregate rates should be read as benchmark observations, not production incident rates.
- Threats to validity: Synthetic/controlled tasks, limited harnesses and model backends, no public executable artifact, and configuration-specific case coverage limit generalization.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: Paper and TeX source are available from arXiv; code, data, and executable benchmark package are unknown/unreleased in v1.
- Environment or compute requirements: Unknown; the paper evaluates 6,560 runs across remote and local agent configurations, but does not provide a reproducible setup package.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Recreate a small crossed matrix of harnesses and backends over a few synthetic tasks, with host-side checks for tool calls, artifacts, state changes, and controlled-service receipts; this is an editorial design suggestion, not a paper result.
- Blocking unknowns: Exact case files, verifier implementation, licenses, model prompts, inference cost, and artifact release status.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The separation of task completion from safety is operationally clear: 97.38% of unsafe runs still met their completion predicate, and the paper reports materially different rates for the same induction strategy across risk carriers.
- Weakest assumption: The benchmark's prespecified unsafe signals and controlled environments may not cover the full failure distribution or real-world consequences of deployed enterprise agents.
- Stated limitations: The authors state that targets/assets are synthetic or controlled and that no code, data, or executable artifacts accompany v1; future work will broaden domains and automate construction.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: The results do not establish production incident rates, prove that one harness or model is universally safer, or demonstrate that lifecycle evidence alone prevents unsafe execution.
