# OSReward: Instituting Standardized Evaluation for Cross-Platform Computer-Use Reward Models

## Identity

- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28609
- Version/date: arXiv v1, submitted 2026-07-30; marked work in progress.
- Authors: Qiushi Sun et al. (23 authors).
- Project/code/data/model: https://os-copilot.github.io/OSReward-Home/

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: Can a VLM judge be trusted to decide whether a computer-use agent actually completed a task?
- Track/gap: `agent-systems` / `agent-evaluation`.
- Why now: Agent evaluation increasingly depends on model judges, while the site lacks a dedicated reading on judge reliability, false-success bias, and evaluation cost.

## Claim map

- Problem: Rule-based verifiers do not cover open-ended trajectories, but model judges may be unreliable.
- Main claim: Current VLM judges share a leniency bias and degrade sharply on hard trajectories; a specialized open reward model can improve the cost–accuracy trade-off.
- Method: Build 1,019 human-gold trajectories across web, mobile, Windows, and Ubuntu; evaluate 27 judges; derive hard and multi-axis subsets; train OS-Shepherd on about 100K annotated judgments.
- Novelty: The benchmark evaluates the trajectory judge itself across platforms instead of treating the judge as a trusted component.

## Evidence audit

- Dataset: 1,019 trajectories, with three independent annotations and senior meta-review on disagreements; the paper reports about 800 human-hours.
- Key result: On OSReward-Hard the best judge falls below 70% and the mean falls to 52%; false-success acceptance is the dominant shared error.
- Baselines: 27 general VLM judges plus comparisons with existing specialized CUA reward work.
- Ablations: input modality, text history, judge prompting/ensembling, cost, model scale, and transfer to existing benchmarks.
- Threats: v1 is explicitly work in progress; some comparisons use incompatible formats; the benchmark is limited to the collected applications, platforms, and task distribution.

## Reproducibility

- The paper states that benchmark trajectories, OS-Shepherd-100K, 9B/35B models, and code are released.
- Full model training requires substantial compute; the paper reports 32 NVIDIA H200 GPUs for training runs.
- Smallest useful reproduction: run selected released judges on a stratified OSReward/OSReward-Hard subset and measure false-success errors and cost.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: carefully adjudicated cross-platform gold labels expose a consistent judge failure rather than only another leaderboard.
- Weakest assumption: the benchmark's task/application mix represents the open-ended trajectories a production agent will encounter.
- Unsupported interpretation: the results do not establish that one learned judge can replace deterministic environment checks where those checks are available.
