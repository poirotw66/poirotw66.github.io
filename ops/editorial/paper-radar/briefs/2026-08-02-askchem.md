# AskChem: Claim-Centered Infrastructure for Chemistry Literature Synthesis

## Identity

- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28618
- Version/date: arXiv v1, submitted 2026-07-30.
- Authors: Bing Yan, Gregory Wolfe, Stefano Martiniani, Kyunghyun Cho.
- Live system: https://askchem.org
- MIT source: https://github.com/bingyan4science/askchem
- CC-BY index snapshot: https://huggingface.co/datasets/bing-yan/askchem

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: What changes when a retrieval system indexes provenance-carrying claims instead of documents or chunks?
- Track/gap: `retrieval-systems` / `production-rag`.
- Why now: The architecture connects evidence provenance, graph relations, taxonomy, REST/SDK/MCP access, and an openly inspectable production system.

## Claim map

- Problem: Paper-ranking interfaces leave evidence extraction, verification, and cross-paper synthesis to the reader or agent.
- Main claim: A claim-centered index can improve citation resolvability and relevance while supporting human and agent access at corpus scale.
- Method: Extract typed atomic claims with DOI and quote/evidence locators; organize them through faceted and living taxonomies plus an evidence graph; expose hybrid retrieval through shared APIs.
- Novelty: Provenance-carrying claims become the persistent retrieval unit rather than a transient answer-generation artifact.

## Evidence audit

- Scale: 2.4M claims from 147K papers, 307K taxonomy nodes, and 171K evidence edges.
- Benchmark: 30 chemistry synthesis questions across aggregation, temporal tracking, and contradiction surfacing.
- Baselines: LLM-only, AskChem-grounded, Paperclip, Edison Scientific, and NotebookLM Deep Research.
- Key result: 100% DOI resolvability for AskChem-grounded answers versus 88.3% for LLM-only; AskChem also reports the highest citation density, while Edison produces more grounded quantitative detail and a slightly higher on-topic rate.
- Threats: Only 30 questions; the study measures groundedness more directly than factual accuracy or user utility; retrieval gain is not isolated; LLM-extracted claims and graph relations can be wrong.

## Reproducibility

- Source, index snapshot, benchmark endpoint, prompts, rerun script, REST API, SDK, and MCP access are reported as available.
- Smallest useful reproduction: rerun a subset of public benchmark questions, validate DOIs, inspect claim-to-source locators, and compare chunk-level retrieval.
- Blocking unknown: operational cost and update latency at full corpus scale require separate measurement.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: architecture, provenance, benchmark artifacts, and a live system are available together.
- Weakest assumption: DOI resolvability and citation density are sufficient proxies for useful scientific synthesis.
- Unsupported interpretation: provenance makes claims auditable, not automatically semantically correct.
