# NiyamAI: Intent-Bound Agents with Cryptographically Verifiable Guardrails

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: Can an agent's tool-call policy be externally verified, and what latency and trust assumptions does that add to the execution boundary?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: `agent-systems` / `agent-security`.
- Gap it fills: It connects intent contracts, isolated policy judgment, zero-knowledge proofs, and tool execution gating in one security architecture.
- Why now: The 2026-08-07 preprint reports a concrete proof-before-execution design and measures both guardrail quality and cryptographic overhead on 2,000 Agent-SafetyBench scenarios.
- Existing coverage and duplication risk: Extends `43-enterprise-ai-agent-security`, `12-agents4d-runtime-risks`, `14-agent-trajectory-sentinel`, and the HarnessSafe/AgentChaos candidates. Duplication risk is medium; the distinctive angle is verifiability of enforcement rather than attack coverage alone.

## Claim map

- Problem: Prompt-level or same-process policy checks can be difficult for a third party to verify and may be bypassed or misrepresented at the tool boundary.
- Main claim: NiyamAI binds allowed tools and constraints in an intent contract, has an isolated judge validate each tool call, and requires verification of a zk-SNARK proof before execution.
- Method: Commit the session intent with SHA-256; intercept tool calls; classify them with an isolated judge; generate an EZKL proof for approved calls; verify the proof; then execute the tool.
- What is genuinely new: It treats a guardrail decision as a verifiable execution artifact, not only a hidden model verdict or local policy log.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: 2,000 Agent-SafetyBench scenarios evaluated with 5-fold stratified cross-validation; the benchmark adaptation and split construction require inspection.
- Benchmarks and metrics: F1 88.5% with 1.1% false-positive rate, bootstrap 95% CI reported as [85.19%, 91.88%] for the stated N=1000 bootstrap sample; paired McNemar comparisons against NeMo Guardrails, Llama Prompt Guard 2, and GPT-OSS-Safeguard.
- Baselines: NeMo Guardrails, Meta Llama Prompt Guard 2, and OpenAI GPT-OSS-Safeguard; the abstract describes zero-shot baseline distinctions.
- Ablations: The abstract reports classifier/proof overhead and paired wins/losses; component ablations, judge isolation tests, proof-system settings, and failure-mode analysis remain to be checked in the PDF.
- Statistical uncertainty: Bootstrap interval and McNemar tests are stated in the abstract, but the full fold-level results, class balance, threshold selection, and multiple-comparison handling require verification.
- Threats to validity: The authors explicitly note that the classifier is adapted to Agent-SafetyBench and compared with zero-shot baselines. This is not evidence of general production attack resistance or universal cryptographic trust.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: No stable public repository, model checkpoint, full configuration, proof circuit, or dataset license package was found during the scan.
- Environment or compute requirements: An isolated judge model, tool interception layer, EZKL/zk-SNARK proving and verification stack, and inference access; proof generation adds a reported 2,260.6 ± 218.4 ms per approved action and verification 53.1 ± 11.8 ms.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Recreate a single intent contract and deterministic tool-call policy with a toy circuit, then measure proof generation/verification separately from judge latency before attempting benchmark reproduction.
- Blocking unknowns: Circuit design, proving parameters, hardware, judge model/version, tool policy schema, benchmark preprocessing, all fold results, and code/license availability remain unknown.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The paper makes enforcement externally checkable in principle and reports the cost of placing proof verification on the critical path.
- Weakest assumption: A judge model's output can be faithfully encoded into a cryptographic proof without turning the proof into a proof only of the judge's own potentially unsafe classification.
- Stated limitations: The abstract itself bounds interpretation to an Agent-SafetyBench-adapted classifier against zero-shot baselines; deployment overhead and artifact availability are unresolved.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: A verified proof does not prove the intent policy is correct, the judge is unbiased, the benchmark represents production attacks, or approved tool actions are safe in their external effects.
