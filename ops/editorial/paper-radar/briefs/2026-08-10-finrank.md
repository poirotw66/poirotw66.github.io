# FinRank: An Evidence-Grounded Benchmark for Financial Question Answering and Retrieval over SEC Filings

## Claim map

- Problem: Financial filings repeat near-identical language across firms and reporting periods, so answer correctness alone can hide evidence misattribution.
- Main claim: FinRank provides 1,185 manually authored QA records from 10-K/10-Q filings of 22 companies, with gold passages and 6,021 curated hard negatives, and shows that these negatives reduce pairwise accuracy by 13.0–20.5 percentage points versus random negatives.
- Method: Evaluate first-stage retrieval on a 5,230-passage pooled corpus and reranking/discrimination on per-record gold-plus-hard-negative sets, stratified by filing, sector, difficulty, reasoning type, and evidence scope.
- What is genuinely new: A released benchmark asset makes provenance discrimination and hard-negative suppression first-class tasks, with per-passage IDs, metadata, a repair log, and reproducible baseline scripts.

## Evidence audit

- Dataset: 1,185 records over 2024–2025 10-K/10-Q filings from 22 US companies in pharmaceuticals, oil and gas, and automotive; 69% include hand-authored query decompositions.
- Baselines: TF-IDF, BM25, all-mpnet-base-v2, a cross-encoder over dense top-20, bge-large-en-v1.5, a finance-adapted embedder, and e5-mistral-7b-instruct under a uniform 512-token truncation.
- Main result: The strongest evaluated embedder reaches 44.8% Recall@10 on the pooled stress-test corpus; metadata-filtered BM25 lifts Recall@10 from 32.1% to 55.0% but can exclude gold passages because the pool stores first-occurrence metadata.
- Ablations and checks: Random, held-out ticker, leave-one-sector-out, year, and document-type splits; query-rewrite ablation; hard-versus-random-negative contrast; deterministic normalization and target-versus-realized distribution audit.
- Uncertainty and limitations: Five annotators worked from shared criteria with sampled review but no formal inter-annotator agreement. The pooled corpus is curated positives plus distractors, not full filing text; 88% of records are 10-K, 75% are 2025, and answer generation/faithfulness are future work.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: Public dataset, evaluation harness, baseline outputs, split assignments, validator, repair log, hard-negative taxonomy, and summary are linked from the official repository. The dataset is CC BY-NC 4.0 and underlying SEC filings are not redistributed wholesale.
- Environment or compute requirements: Python baseline scripts and common retrieval/reranking libraries; exact full-run compute and deployment cost are not stated as a production recipe.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Run the validator and baseline scripts, then reproduce the pooled Recall@10 and hard-versus-random-negative contrast before extending the corpus to full filing chunks.
- Blocking unknowns: Full-document chunking, multimodal tables, live SEC refresh, query leakage, inter-annotator agreement, and end-to-end answer faithfulness remain outside the released baseline evidence.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The benchmark demonstrates that metadata and hard-negative design materially change what “retrieval quality” means for evidence-sensitive enterprise QA.
- Weakest assumption: A curated passage pool is useful for controlled discrimination but may not estimate first-stage retrieval against all text, tables, footnotes, ACLs, and document versions in production.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: FinRank does not establish a universal best retriever, prove that metadata filtering is safe for every corpus, or validate automated investment decisions.
