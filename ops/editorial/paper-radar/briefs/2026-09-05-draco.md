# DRACO: Fine-Grained Credit Assignment with Dynamic Rubrics for Long-Horizon Agent Training

## Identity

- Stable ID: `arxiv:2609.04094`.
- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04094
- Authors: Shubham Gandhi, Saurabh Goyal, Kiran Kate, and Yara Rizk.
- Venue or review status: arXiv v1 submitted 2026-09-03; subjects cs.AI, cs.LG, and cs.SE.
- DOI / OpenReview / arXiv aliases: `10.48550/arXiv.2609.04094`; no separate venue record located.
- Code / model / data: https://github.com/IBM/draco.

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: How can long-horizon agent training assign useful credit when the environment has no programmatic verifier?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: It fills `agent-systems` / `tool-use-reliability` with a training-time method for turning trajectory-level rubric judgments into step-level learning signals.
- Gap it fills: Existing evaluation candidates focus on judging and deployment qualification; DRACO addresses the credit-assignment bottleneck that makes agent improvement difficult after a run fails.
- Why now: The paper reports gains on AppWorld and out-of-domain Tau-Bench without requiring a frontier judge at evaluation time.

## Claim map

- Problem: Sparse or trajectory-level rewards provide weak learning signals across tens of tool-use steps, especially when verifiable rewards are unavailable.
- Main claim: Dynamically generated rubrics plus closed-form redistribution improve GRPO training over base, sparse-ground-truth, and other rubric settings.
- Method: Generate rubrics during training, score completed trajectories, annotate responsible steps, and distribute the judgment into differentiated per-step advantages without a trained attribution module.
- What is genuinely new: The method couples evolving capability-aware rubrics with explicit step attribution while keeping the attribution rule closed-form.

## Evidence audit

- Benchmarks and training: AppWorld with Qwen3.6-27B and Qwen2.5-32B settings; out-of-domain Tau-Bench evaluation; the public repository contains training/eval code and launcher documentation.
- Metrics: AppWorld task metrics and Tau-Bench success rate across sparse/static/dynamic/credit ablations and model settings.
- Baselines: Base model, GRPO with sparse ground-truth reward, static rubric, dynamic rubric, and dynamic-plus-credit variants.
- Statistical uncertainty: Reported gains include +15.9 points over base and +5.3 over sparse ground-truth GRPO on AppWorld, but training runs, judge dependence, and benchmark variance need independent re-runs.
- Threats to validity: Most experiments use a single frozen judge; rubric fidelity has no independent signal; AppWorld/Tau-Bench may not represent production workflows.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: Public IBM repository with `training` and `evals`, Apache-2.0 repository metadata, launcher documentation, and explicit secret separation.
- Environment or compute requirements: Model weights and AppWorld data are external; GPU training, an OpenAI-compatible rubric-judge endpoint for several settings, and Weights & Biases-compatible logging are expected.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Run the dynamic versus dynamic-plus-credit ablation on one smaller model and a reduced AppWorld slice, then inspect which steps receive redistributed advantage.
- Blocking unknowns: Exact checkpoints, judge prompts, total compute, sensitivity to judge model drift, and whether credit attribution improves real deployment outcomes rather than benchmark scores.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The combination of dynamic rubrics and credit assignment improves in-domain and out-of-domain results without a learned attribution module.
- Weakest assumption: A rubric judge can identify responsible steps accurately enough that its errors do not dominate the training signal.
- Stated limitations: The paper notes no independent rubric-fidelity signal and reliance on a mostly frozen judge; human agreement and chance-corrected validation remain open.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: The results do not prove that dynamic rubrics are reliable for arbitrary tasks or that benchmark improvements imply safer autonomous behavior.
