# SkillZip: Evaluation-Free Skill Compression for Self-Evolving Agents by Discovering Reusable Structure

- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.11079`.
- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11079

- Venue or review status: arXiv v1, submitted 2026-08-11; no venue or review record identified.

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: When an agent's skills grow through appended fixes and procedures, how can we remove repetition without deleting rare rules or silently changing the tool contract?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: It fits `agent-systems` / `tool-use-reliability` and complements the archive's SkillProx and Catastrophic Remembering candidates with a structural, write-time compression strategy.
- Gap it fills: Skill lifecycle management, instruction growth, contract-preserving compression, and cost-aware self-evolution.
- Why now: The paper is a fresh response to a practical failure mode in agent systems: skill memory grows through append-only updates and eventually becomes expensive to inject and maintain.

## Claim map

- Problem: Self-evolving agents accumulate repeated requirements, copied action sequences, warnings, and exceptions in skills that become costly and hard to maintain.
- Main claim: A typed minimum-description-length objective with hard coverage constraints can compress skills without evaluation rollouts while preserving triggers, workflow edges, tool requirements, obligations, and output fields.
- Method: Extract reusable structure, share repeated rules at the narrowest valid scope, retain residual exceptions, and support one-shot compression plus continual Zip-on-Write updates.
- What is genuinely new: The contract/residual formulation and coverage-preserving structural sharing applied to agent skills rather than flat prompt compression.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: The abstract says comprehensive experiments evaluate compression, generalizability, and cost overhead, but does not specify the benchmark families, skill corpora, or task counts.
- Benchmarks and metrics: Need the full paper for token/latency savings, behavior retention, generalization, rare-rule preservation, and comparison baselines.
- Baselines: Evaluation-guided compression and generic prompt compression are described conceptually; exact implementations and baseline parity require verification.
- Ablations: Verify the effect of hard coverage constraints, typed contract fields, scope sharing, and Zip-on-Write versus full-history replay.
- Statistical uncertainty: No numerical result or variance is present in the abstract; do not treat “superiority” as verified until tables and repeated runs are inspected.
- Threats to validity: Structural extraction errors, hidden semantic dependencies, rare exceptions not represented in the coverage schema, and compression-time model behavior can all cause silent regressions.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: No public code, skill corpus, or benchmark artifact was located during this scan.
- Environment or compute requirements: A structured skill parser/extractor, contract validator, deterministic compression optimizer, and task replay harness for behavior regression.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Create skills with repeated rules, shared procedures, tool contracts, and rare exceptions; compare raw append-only, generic compression, and SkillZip-like compression on token cost and held-out task behavior.
- Blocking unknowns: Extraction prompt, structural schema, coverage checker, optimizer details, benchmark composition, and exact cost measurements.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The hard coverage constraint makes preservation an explicit invariant instead of relying solely on a post-compression evaluation set.
- Weakest assumption: Every behaviorally relevant dependency can be represented by the extracted triggers, edges, obligations, and output fields; implicit interaction effects may escape the contract.
- Stated limitations: Verify in the full paper; the abstract does not expose the corpus, failure cases, or artifact status.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: Evaluation-free compression does not mean regression-free compression, and the abstract does not establish safe use for arbitrary skills or production tool policies.
