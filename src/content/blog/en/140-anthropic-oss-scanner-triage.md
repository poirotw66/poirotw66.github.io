In October 2026, Anthropic launched OSS Scanner, allowing eligible open-source projects that opt in to receive model-generated vulnerability reports directly. The fast track has no human review or triage before delivery, so reports may be wrong. Anthropic is also keeping its existing coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) path, where external security researchers validate findings before maintainers are notified. The consequential change is not simply another scanner: once vulnerability discovery outpaces validation capacity, someone must take responsibility for the first review.

> **Huahua in one sentence**
>
> Models can increase the supply of vulnerability candidates, but unvalidated reports transfer triage work to maintainers as well.

## Candidate volume exceeds review capacity

Anthropic says that over the previous six months its models produced more than 29,000 candidate vulnerabilities while its team could manually review and triage only about 6,000. Anthropic also says some maintainers who received initial reports asked for a bulk submission of all unverified candidates and proposed patches; by launch, nearly 5,000 unvalidated reports had been sent directly at maintainers' request. These are activity figures reported by Anthropic. They describe its workload and workflow choices, not an independent quality evaluation.

OSS Scanner formalizes that “send the candidates and let the project assess them” option. Anthropic says reports are generated by models and may include reproduction steps, an explanation, a possible bisection for when a bug was introduced, and a candidate patch when available. Projects must determine whether a report reproduces, fits their threat model, duplicates a known issue, and warrants action.

## Two disclosure paths place responsibility differently

Anthropic is maintaining two paths that should not be conflated:

1. **Standard CVD:** Models produce candidates, then external security researchers reproduce issues, assess whether they are real vulnerabilities, and rate severity before validated reports go to maintainers. This path absorbs much of the verification work and is better suited to projects that cannot process a large stream of raw candidates themselves.
2. **OSS Scanner fast track:** Core maintainers of eligible projects opt in by submitting configuration. Model-generated reports go directly to them without prior human review. Projects can provide a threat model, severity rubric, and deduplication preferences, and they can pause or leave. They still own reproduction, classification, and scheduling after reports arrive.

This choice is not right for every open-source project. Anthropic's [FAQ](https://red.anthropic.com/oss-scanner/) says it will assess eligibility case by case and positions the service for projects already able to handle verified high or critical vulnerability reports. If maintainers cannot keep up with credible high-severity reports, adding unvalidated candidates may bury important signals in the queue.

> **Huahua's engineering note**
>
> A reproducer or candidate patch does not make a vulnerability confirmed; reproduce it in a controlled environment, then assess impact against the project's own threat model.

## What the 97-finding sample does—and does not—show

Anthropic says it asked the expert penetration testers who review its CVD findings to check 97 high and critical findings from an early OSS Scanner version across 48 projects. Eighty-five met Anthropic's CVD bar; 11 of the remaining findings were real but duplicated or otherwise not reportable, and one was invalid. This result is notable, but it is a **severity-selected sample of 97 findings**. It is not a random sample, an overall precision estimate for the 29,000-plus candidate pool, or a measure of all unreviewed direct reports.

Anthropic's public [CVD dashboard](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/cvd/) and [disclosure ledger](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/cvd/ledger/) cover a separate, broader coordinated disclosure program. As of October 2, 2026, the dashboard lists 6,123 findings reviewed by external security firms and 5,674 confirmed as valid, alongside direct reports to maintainers that may contain false positives. Those totals span multiple models and CVD workflows; they cannot be used as an accuracy estimate for projects newly enrolling in OSS Scanner. The dashboard and ledger document the broader program's review sources and disclosure states, but do not independently evaluate the new service's full candidate stream.

Independent evidence is still missing on the new service's overall candidate precision, false-positive rates by severity or project type, whether maintainer time is reduced, and whether reports ultimately lead to fixes or fewer recurring vulnerabilities. Those gaps matter when deciding whether to enroll; a report that includes a reproducer or a high model-assigned severity is not itself a verified conclusion.

## Maintainers need a triage contract they can sustain

Before enrolling, a project can make the workflow explicit:

- **Assign an owner and backup.** A monitored security address needs a responsible reviewer and a fallback for absences or overdue reports. Do not route reports only to an unattended personal inbox.
- **Define the threat model and severity rubric.** State which inputs, deployment conditions, and trust boundaries are in scope, as well as which issues do not constitute an exploitable risk in this project. This can guide model reports, but it does not replace verification.
- **Preserve an evidence trail.** Record the report ID, version or commit, reproduction environment, preconditions, impact, deduplication result, remediation decision, and disclosure state. Keep “model asserted,” “maintainer reproduced,” and “patched” as separate statuses.
- **Set capacity and exit conditions.** Estimate how many reports the team can review each week, and pause intake if the queue ages, false-positive costs become too high, or remediation capacity is unavailable. Receiving more leads does not guarantee faster fixes.

The point is not to treat the model's judgment as a new authority. Every additional lead needs an owner, a verification step, and a terminal status. OSS Scanner turns a request that some maintainers had made individually into an opt-in delivery model. For critical infrastructure projects with capacity to validate reports, it may shorten the path from candidate to maintainer. For projects with limited triage resources, unreviewed reports may become another burden. The outcomes worth tracking therefore extend beyond candidate counts: reproducibility, deduplication cost, maintainer response time, fixes completed, and the share of projects that pause intake.
