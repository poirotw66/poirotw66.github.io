# CorporateBench: Large-Scale Q&A Benchmarking with Temporal Knowledge Bases

## Identity

- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.27391`.
- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27391
- Authors: Sil Hamilton, Albert Yu Sun, Oscar J. Romero, Carl-Leander Henneking, David Mimno, Bishan Yang, and Igor Labutov.
- Venue or review status: arXiv v1 submitted 2026-08-27; no separate review record verified in this scan.
- DOI / OpenReview / arXiv aliases: `10.48550/arXiv.2608.27391`; no separate identifier located.
- Code / model / data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/epiq-ai-labs/corporatebench; the paper states that data and code are released there.

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: What happens to enterprise retrieval and reasoning when the knowledge base reaches hundreds of thousands of temporally linked documents?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: CorporateBench directly fills `retrieval-systems` / `production-rag` with a scale- and time-aware enterprise benchmark.
- Gap it fills: Existing RAG benchmarks are often small, static, or weakly connected; this benchmark tests information extraction and knowledge-base querying over internally consistent corporate communication networks.
- Why now: Enterprise RAG claims increasingly depend on realistic corpus scale, temporal consistency, and cross-document reasoning rather than a handful of clean passages.

## Claim map

- Problem: Private enterprise corpora are difficult to release, while simplified synthetic benchmarks do not capture scale and document interdependence.
- Main claim: Procedurally generated, human-validated corporate communication networks can provide a large-scale benchmark with verifiable ground truth and temporal consistency.
- Method: Generate four firms ranging from 12 to 10,000 employees, sample a temporally evolving knowledge base into 263,466 documents, and evaluate extraction, topic, integrated QA, and knowledge-base QA tasks.
- What is genuinely new: The benchmark makes corpus scale and organizational consistency explicit variables instead of treating enterprise RAG as ordinary long-context QA.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: Four procedurally generated firms with 263,466 total synthetic documents and a 90-day simulated quarter; the paper reports human validation of the benchmark.
- Benchmarks and metrics: Information extraction, knowledge-base querying, topic classification, and integrated QA; results are reported across corpora and five recent LLMs.
- Baselines: Multiple recent LLMs, TF-IDF baselines for topic classification, and separate RAG/knowledge-base tool configurations are described in the experimental appendices.
- Ablations: The paper varies corpus scale, QA method, training/example conditions, and relationship/topic evaluation settings.
- Statistical uncertainty: Human validation and per-task results help, but the main scale result is still a benchmark observation on procedurally generated data rather than a production deployment estimate.
- Threats to validity: The corpus is primarily email, the simulated period is one quarter, and documents are generated by Claude Haiku 4.5; real Slack, Teams, specifications, spreadsheets, and organizational evolution may behave differently.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: The paper links a Hugging Face dataset and states that data and code are released; dataset, code, and model terms must be checked directly before reuse.
- Environment or compute requirements: Reproduction requires the released corpus, a vector or relational retrieval setup, model/API access, and enough storage and compute to process enterprise-scale document counts.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Download one firm and one scale tier, run the provided extraction/KB-QA configuration, and compare performance as the document count crosses the reported scale transitions.
- Blocking unknowns: Exact artifact version, complete code path, generation seeds, prompt files, retrieval/index build cost, and independent human-corpus replication remain to be verified.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The benchmark keeps cross-document logical consistency while scaling to 263,466 documents, making corpus size a measurable stressor rather than a vague “long context” label.
- Weakest assumption: Procedurally generated email networks are a useful lower bound for the messiness, permissions, formats, and institutional drift of real enterprise knowledge bases.
- Stated limitations: The paper limits scope to communication networks and simplified document types, models only a 90-day period, and acknowledges LLM-generated artifacts.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: The results do not prove that a particular retriever, model, or RAG architecture will win on a real company's corpus, nor that scale alone explains every deployment failure.
