# DocMemo: Dynamic Evidence Discovery via Probabilistic Memory-Guided Retrieval

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: How can a long-document RAG system recover after its first page selection misses the evidence instead of repeatedly trusting a static top-k set?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: `retrieval-systems` / `rag-evaluation`.
- Gap it fills: It connects cross-round evidence recall, retrieval state, unanswerable decisions, and multimodal page/region evidence in a measurable long-document workflow.
- Why now: The 2026-08-07 paper reports controlled comparisons across MMLongBench-Doc, LongDocURL, and PaperTab, with public code and explicit memory/retrieval ablations.
- Existing coverage and duplication risk: Complements `03-RAG-ANYTHING`, `07-GraphRAG-vs-RAG`, `13-bm25-wins-at-scale`, `18-finrank-evidence-grounded-rag`, and the TREC RAG evaluation path. Duplication risk is medium; the focus should be dynamic evidence discovery, not generic multimodal RAG.

## Claim map

- Problem: Static retrieval commits to a page pool before reasoning, while iterative retrieval often lacks an explicit state model for how page relevance changes across rounds.
- Main claim: A tri-level memory—Document Schema Memory, Page Belief Memory, and Question Episodic Memory—plus Bayesian belief updates and Thompson sampling improves long-document evidence discovery.
- Method: Encode pages, maintain document structure and query history, update page posteriors from reasoning feedback, sample and rerank candidate pages, accumulate evidence, and use adaptive-granularity visual regions for dense tables/figures.
- What is genuinely new: The paper turns cross-round reasoning feedback into a probabilistic retrieval state rather than treating each round as an independent search.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: MMLongBench-Doc (1,082 questions over 135 long documents), LongDocURL (2,325 QA pairs over 396 PDFs), and PaperTab (393 questions over 307 scientific papers).
- Benchmarks and metrics: Accuracy, evidence recall, and all-hit rate; the paper reports overall averages of 77.6 across the three benchmarks and category-level MMLongBench-Doc results.
- Baselines: Proprietary and open multimodal models, SimpleDoc, MoLoRAG, MLDocRAG, AgenticOCR, and DocLens; a controlled same-backbone/retriever/rounds comparison is also reported.
- Ablations: Removing all memory modules reduces MMLongBench-Doc accuracy from 71.3 to 68.5; removing Bayesian updating reduces it to 68.8; separate ablations cover Thompson sampling and adaptive granularity. Evidence recall rises from 28.32% after round one to 69.56% after round three, while all-hit rate rises from 12.90% to 58.05%.
- Statistical uncertainty: The paper reports automatic GPT-4.1 binary judging plus a human verification appendix; confidence intervals, judge agreement, and cost variance require detailed review.
- Threats to validity: Results depend on page-rendering, OCR/visual embedding, Qwen3.5-VL-9B, ColQwen2.5, MinerU, A100 GPUs, and benchmark annotations. Automatic judge outcomes and three long-document datasets may not predict enterprise corpus behavior.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: Public GitHub repository with environment, data layout, embedding, summary, memory, and QA commands. Dataset and model licenses, exact commit, and released result files require verification.
- Environment or compute requirements: Python 3.11, CUDA/A100-class hardware, vLLM, Qwen3.5-VL-9B, ColQwen2.5-v0.2, MinerU, rendered PDF pages, and multiple preprocessing/inference stages.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Run the repository on a small MMLongBench subset with three retrieval rounds, compare static retrieval versus DocMemo, and reproduce evidence recall/all-hit curves before full accuracy evaluation.
- Blocking unknowns: Exact hardware hours and cost, dataset download integrity, prompt/judge versions, checkpoint hashes, and whether all appendix experiments are runnable from the repository remain to be checked.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The controlled ablations and cross-round evidence curves connect the proposed memory components to a concrete recovery behavior rather than only reporting a final answer score.
- Weakest assumption: Bayesian page beliefs and LLM feedback are calibrated enough that accumulated state improves exploration instead of reinforcing early retrieval errors.
- Stated limitations: The evidence is tied to long-document visual QA, automatic judging, and a particular multimodal stack; broader enterprise retrieval, access control, freshness, and cost are not established.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: DocMemo does not prove general RAG reliability, universal cost savings, or improved citation faithfulness for arbitrary production corpora.
