# RAGSieve: self-referenced contrast for RAG knowledge-poison detection

## Identity

- Stable ID: `arxiv:2608.13010`.
- Canonical URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13010
- Authors: Xinlong Xu and Yoshua Y. Li.
- Venue or review status: arXiv v1, submitted 2026-08-13; no venue or review record identified.
- DOI / OpenReview / arXiv aliases: arXiv-issued DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2608.13010`; no separate identifier identified.
- Code / model / data: https://github.com/XrazyMee/RAGSieve; MIT license. The repository documents demo/tests, dataset preparation, index construction, and model requirements; full attack-generation implementations and deployment-grade scale remain to be checked.

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: Can a RAG system detect suspicious retrieval promotion without a trusted clean corpus, poison labels, or a fixed global threshold?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: It fills the `retrieval-systems` / `production-rag` gap with a concrete ingestion-time and query-time defense against a production-relevant supply-chain failure.
- Gap it fills: Retrieval integrity, corpus poisoning detection, local anomaly baselines, and the difference between ranking promotion and factual truth.
- Why now: The 2026-08-13 paper pairs a measured defense with a public artifact, making it suitable for an engineering-critical Deep Read rather than a security-news summary.

## Claim map

- Problem: Poisoned documents can promote attacker-chosen claims; trusted-reference detectors, attack-specific artifacts, and global thresholds are brittle under changing corpus topology.
- Main claim: Self-referenced local contrast can detect suspicious promotion online and offline without poison labels or a trusted clean corpus.
- Method: RAGSieve-Query compares top-five results with ranks 6–20 for query-local contrast; RAGSieve-Graph compares each document with semantically similar but lexically distinct local neighbors; joint deployment screens both ingestion and query paths.
- What is genuinely new: The method uses the inspected retrieval system/corpus as its reference and separates query-local from corpus-local signals. It is not a truth verifier and does not eliminate the need for provenance, access control, or content review.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: Natural Questions, HotpotQA, and MS MARCO; the paper evaluates six poisoning constructions and three dense retrievers.
- Benchmarks and metrics: RSQ reports 95.2% AUROC and 82.2% poison detection at 5% clean-document removal versus GMTP's 81.1%/52.5%; RSG reports 93.3%/79.8% versus CleanBase's 79.4%/37.6%; joint deployment reduces attack success from 67.4% to 14.0% while retaining 41.3 F1 on unpoisoned retrieval. Online overhead is reported around 447 ms and an offline 128,544-document run around 46.54 s.
- Baselines: GMTP for query-time detection and CleanBase for corpus-local comparison; three dense retrievers and six attack constructions provide useful coverage.
- Ablations: The two modules, local reference choices, poisoning rates, and unpoisoned retrieval behavior are compared in the HTML; table-level checks are required before turning the reported headline into a deployment estimate.
- Statistical uncertainty: AUROC and attack-success figures are experimental results on selected datasets/attacks; confidence intervals, seed variance, and natural-poison prevalence are not yet summarized here.
- Threats to validity: The detector targets promotion patterns, not factual correctness; sparse or coordinated attacks, corpus changes, retriever families outside the tested set, and adaptive attackers can evade the assumptions.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: MIT-licensed GitHub repository with README, demo, tests, data/index instructions, and model setup notes; arXiv source/HTML are available.
- Environment or compute requirements: Dense embedding/index construction, dataset downloads, model weights, and CUDA are recommended for the full path; exact hardware and runtime cost need a reproduction audit.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Run the repository demo on one dataset, inject controlled promotion attacks, compare RSQ/RSG with a clean baseline, and measure benign retrieval quality, detection latency, and false positives under corpus updates.
- Blocking unknowns: Full attack generator coverage, exact commits and environment lock, natural-poison calibration, false-positive cost, multi-tenant authorization interactions, and adaptive-attacker evaluation.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: One framework provides complementary local references for both query-time and corpus-time screening, with a public artifact that makes the key numbers inspectable.
- Weakest assumption: Local semantic neighborhoods remain a useful reference when poisoning is coordinated, sparse, or already widespread.
- Stated limitations: The paper explicitly distinguishes promotion detection from truth verification and leaves adaptive/coordinated attacks as open problems.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: RAGSieve does not prove that retrieved claims are true, that poisoned content is fully removed, or that the measured latency and ASR transfer to production-scale multi-tenant systems.
