## Editorial fit

- Reader question: How can an automated agent-improvement loop remember what is still broken, what was already tried, and whether a fix actually held?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: `agent-systems` / `agent-evaluation`.
- Gap it fills: It turns harness improvement from candidate-level history into explicit issue lifecycle and intervention-outcome state.
- Why now: The preprint provides a concrete full-code optimization loop across Tau-Bench, ALFWorld, TextCraft, WebShop, and ScienceWorld, with matched budgets and held-out evaluation.
- Existing coverage and duplication risk: It complements `08-osreward-agent-evaluation`, `14-agent-trajectory-sentinel`, `19-a2e-agent-auditing-engine`, and harness engineering articles. Duplication risk is medium; the distinct angle is persistent repair state rather than another evaluator or runtime monitor.

## Claim map

- Problem: Candidate-centric optimization scatters failure evidence across agent versions and can repeat failed interventions or lose partial repair progress.
- Main claim: An issue-centric state with stable issue IDs, lifecycle status, evidence, and intervention history improves automated agent design.
- Method: Initialize issue hypotheses from task priors and observed trajectories; associate failures across rounds; select target issues and revision directions; apply focused full-code changes; re-evaluate; and transition issues through active, tentatively-fixed, confirmed-fixed, and regressed states.
- What is genuinely new: The persistent object being optimized is the repair objective itself, not only a prompt, skill, topology, or candidate agent.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: Five interactive benchmarks—Tau-Bench, ALFWorld, TextCraft, WebShop, and ScienceWorld—with representative subsets for the main experiments.
- Benchmarks and metrics: Native task success/score plus within-benchmark interaction efficiency; four backbone models, including DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2, Hy3-Preview, and GPT-5.4.
- Baselines: Handcrafted ReAct-style agent, SkillOpt, Agentic Harness Engineering, Meta-Harness, and DGM-Hyperagents under shared wrappers, splits, interfaces, and scoring scripts.
- Ablations: Removing the persistent issue state or replacing issue-guided revision with candidate-centric policies causes reported drops of up to 40.7%; the main result reports 78.4 average score versus 62.6 for the strongest baseline, a 25.2% relative improvement.
- Statistical uncertainty: The abstract and inspected HTML report averages, matched settings, and ablations; confidence intervals, significance tests, and variance across optimization seeds remain to be checked in the full paper and repository.
- Threats to validity: Main experiments use representative subsets, a fixed ten-iteration optimization budget, model/API availability assumptions, and benchmark-specific wrappers. Public benchmark generalization and optimizer leakage need review.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The method makes repair progress auditable and reports consistent gains across five heterogeneous interactive environments and four backbones.
- Weakest assumption: Stable issue identity and evidence-grounded diagnosis are themselves reliable enough that issue-centric state does not lock the optimizer onto a wrong repair abstraction.
- Stated limitations: The paper's inspected sections emphasize fixed task environments, matched-model protocols, representative subsets, and finite optimization budgets; broader online or adversarial settings remain open.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: ADIAS does not prove universal agent self-improvement, monotonic safety, production cost savings, or that issue state replaces independent regression and safety gates.
