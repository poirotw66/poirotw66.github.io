# VRL-Bench: A Benchmark for Trial-and-Error Learning in Agents

## Editorial fit

- Reader question: When an agent is allowed to retry, does asking it to write a verbal “lesson” actually help, or can the lesson make it worse?
- Why this belongs in the selected track: VRL-Bench turns trial-and-error learning into paired, budgeted experiments where updates can be compared directly against simply retrying.
- Gap it fills: Agent evaluation—fair measurement of within-task learning under finite interaction budgets.
- Why now: Many agent papers claim self-reflection benefits without a clean retry control, making it hard to tell learning from extra attempts.

## Claim map

- Problem: Verbal self-updates and retry budgets are often confounded, so apparent reflection gains may be an artifact of more attempts.
- Main claim: In the evaluated settings, existing verbal updates can hurt relative to retry, while VEX² is the only tested update that shows a positive observed success over retry in all six settings.
- Method: The harness pairs update and retry conditions under finite trial budgets across MiniWoB and WebShop, with fixed visible feedback and matched cases.
- What is genuinely new: It makes “learning from failure” compete against the simplest useful baseline—try again—under the same budget.

## Evidence audit

- Datasets: 2,220 paired cases and 16,946 executions from MiniWoB and WebShop.
- Benchmarks and metrics: Success under six trial budgets, update-versus-retry deltas, and paired bootstrap intervals across three models.
- Baselines: Retry without a verbal update and existing verbal-update strategies.
- Ablations: Six budgets, three models, benchmark/task differences, and update policy variants.
- Statistical uncertainty: The paper reports paired bootstrap intervals and uses matched cases, which makes the retry comparison more informative than an unpaired aggregate.
- Threats to validity: Only three models and two benchmarks, visible feedback, six trials, and no model-call/token accounting; irreversible actions, hidden feedback, and other reset semantics remain open.

## Reproducibility

- Available artifacts and licenses: No verified public repository or artifact link was found in the inspected HTML.
- Environment or compute requirements: MiniWoB/WebShop environments, agent model access, controlled reset and feedback instrumentation, and paired execution logging.
- Smallest useful reproduction: Run one task family with matched retry/update policies at budgets 1–6, log every attempt and feedback token, and report paired success deltas plus intervals.
- Blocking unknowns: Exact environment versions, prompts, model identifiers, update serialization, and cost accounting require source-level verification.

## Critical reading

- Strongest result: The benchmark shows that a more articulate self-update is not automatically better than a retry, and gives a budget-aware way to expose that failure.
- Weakest assumption: Visible benchmark feedback and resettable tasks approximate the feedback and risk profile of real agents operating on partially observable systems.
- Stated limitations: The authors limit the evidence to three models, two benchmarks, six trials, and visible feedback.
- Claims not supported by the evidence: A positive update-over-retry delta in these environments does not establish long-horizon learning or general self-improvement.
